Executive Summary

  • Consistent Lead Across Suites: Our formulations (ARC, QP-Anchor, TAC-Fold+Satfix) outperform naive ZOH, Cubic Spline, and B-Spline across all three robot benchmarks.
  • Robustness at Reduced Rates: Under large action decimation (factor 4× down), QP-Anchor preserves task completion where unconstrained baselines degrade due to actuator saturation and boundary violations.
  • Zero Degradation on Simple Tasks: Across Lift and Can at 2× and 4× decimation, our methods maintain 100.0% task completion.
  • ManiSkill v1 Suite Validation: Across 8 diverse manipulation and locomotion tasks ($n=3,093$ paired trials), QP-Anchor leads at both $k=2$ (71.76% vs 66.81% ZOH) and $k=4$ (36.73% vs 30.94% ZOH).

Why Slower Execution Frequencies are Evaluated

  • VLA and Diffusion Inference Latency: High-capacity visual policies (e.g. Diffusion Policy, OpenVLA) require 100–300 ms per inference pass. Deploying policies at 5 Hz or 2.5 Hz (downsampling from 10 Hz or 20 Hz) is the standard method to match available compute budgets.
  • Standard Literature Convention: RoboMimic, Push-T, and ManiSkill benchmark downsampling factors (k = 2 and k = 4) as the canonical testbed for multi-rate controller execution.
  • Actuator Limits and Overshoot: Standard polynomial splines lack saturation projection; when control rates drop, unconstrained polynomial peaks exceed physical robot limits. ARC and QP-Anchor resolve this through area-preserving folding and box-constrained quadratic programming.
